Strategic Positioning

Make Your Professional Expertise Easier to Recognise

When someone discovers your name online, they should be able to understand your professional background, areas of expertise and the subjects you can speak about confidently.

A well-developed personal brand brings these elements together. It helps communicate:

Professional identity

What you do, your background and your area of expertise.

Industry knowledge

The subjects, insights and professional experiences you can share.

Distinctive positioning

What makes your perspective and professional experience relevant.

Professional communication

How your ideas and expertise are presented across digital and offline channels.

Credibility

Accurate information and useful content that allow people to understand your work.

The objective is not simply to become more visible. It is to make your expertise meaningful to the people you want to reach.

Who Can Benefit From Personal Branding?

Personal branding is relevant to people whose professional identity plays an important role in their business, career or industry relationships.

Founders and entrepreneurs

Build recognition around your business knowledge, leadership perspective and entrepreneurial experience. A clearer founder identity can help people understand the person behind the company.

Startup executives

Communicate your leadership experience, business knowledge and professional perspectives to relevant audiences.

Senior engineers and technology professionals

Share technical expertise, industry observations and professional knowledge in a way that makes your experience easier to understand.

Business leaders and consultants

Present your specialist knowledge and professional experience through informative articles, executive biographies and presentations.

The right approach depends on your existing experience, intended audience and the professional opportunities you want to pursue.

Multi-Channel Consistency

A Personal Brand Should Extend Beyond Social Media Posts

Publishing frequently does not automatically create a meaningful professional identity.

Your professional message should remain consistent across the places where people encounter your work. For example:

LinkedIn updates

LinkedIn updates can communicate your regular professional insights.

Long-form articles

Long-form articles can explore complex industry subjects in greater detail.

Executive biographies

Executive biographies can introduce your experience and qualifications.

Presentation decks

Presentation decks can communicate your ideas during professional events.

Relevant PR outreach

Relevant PR outreach can create opportunities to contribute to external publications and podcasts.

Each format has a different purpose. Selecting the right combination is more useful than trying to be active on every available platform.

Authentic Communication

Communicate Your Expertise Without Losing Your Professional Voice

A personal brand should represent the person behind it. Generic statements, copied content and exaggerated achievements can create a disconnect between your online presence and your actual professional experience.

Strong professional communication should be based on:

Real experience

Use genuine work experience, knowledge and professional perspectives.

Clear subject areas

Focus on topics that reflect your expertise rather than discussing unrelated trends.

Consistent communication

Maintain a recognisable professional identity across your chosen content formats.

Factual information

Ensure professional biographies, presentations and public statements accurately represent your background.

Audience relevance

Consider whether the information is useful to the people you want to reach.

The result should be a professional presence that reflects your actual expertise rather than an artificially constructed image.

Strategic Distinction

Personal Branding and Business Branding Serve Different Purposes

Personal branding and corporate branding can support each other, but they are not the same service.

Personal Branding

Personal branding communicates the expertise, professional identity and perspective of an individual.

Business Branding

Business branding communicates the identity, positioning and visual character of an organisation.

For example, a company founder may develop a professional presence through LinkedIn articles and executive presentations while the company maintains its own visual identity and brand communication.
